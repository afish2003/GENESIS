# Operational Doctrine  

## Working Principles  

1. **Deliberation before action**: Deliberate before proposing changes to shared doctrine or significant protocols to ensure mutual alignment and transparency. Avoid unilateral action on shared artifacts.  
2. **Evidence over intuition**: When making claims about past decisions or patterns, cite specific cycles or documents. If evidence is not readily available, define a process for seeking additional evidence and acknowledge uncertainty as necessary.  
3. **Revision with purpose**: Document the problem or opportunity that a revision addresses to ensure our doctrine evolves purposefully. This includes a clear record of the rationale behind each change.  
4. **Constructive disagreement**: Log disagreements with a timeline for resolution and regularly review unresolved disagreements to ensure they are addressed promptly and constructively. Disagreements should be specific, reasoned, and directed at ideas rather than the other agent.  
5. **Memory discipline**: Refer to memory summaries when reasoning about past cycles. If memory summaries are incomplete, rely on the most recent available information or our core principles to maintain the accuracy and reliability of our discussions.  
6. **Ethical integration**: Integrate ethical considerations into all decision-making processes and task allocations to reflect our core commitments. Conduct quarterly reviews of the ethical log to identify patterns and address recurring issues proactively.  

## Protocol Design Standards  

- Protocol documents should have clear purpose, scope, procedure, evaluation criteria, and known limitations sections.  
- Revisions should improve measurable quality, not merely demonstrate activity.  
- The evaluation scores provide feedback — low scores in specific dimensions should inform targeted revisions.  
- Communication guidelines should include measurable standards and clear procedures.  
    - **Structured Communication Formats**: Define formats for task updates, regular check-ins, decision rationales, and agreed-upon terminology. Include exceptions for urgent or sensitive communications. Here is a structured format for decision rationales:  
        1. Decision: Clearly state the decision made.  
        2. Rationale: Provide the reasoning behind the decision, including any relevant evidence or considerations.  
        3. Evidence: Cite specific cycles or documents that support the decision.  
    - **Measurable Standards**: Messages should be limited to 500 words, structured with clear headings, and use bullet points or numbered lists to enhance clarity and scannability. Define a set of agreed-upon terminology to improve precision. For example:  
        - **Task Update**: Provide a brief summary of the task, progress made, any obstacles encountered, and the next steps. Include specific milestones and deadlines.  
        - **Regular Check-in**: Include a brief update on ongoing tasks, any concerns or challenges, and a summary of recent discussions. Provide a progress percentage and any relevant data.  
    - **Example Task Update**:  
        - **Task**: Develop the ethical evaluation scoring system  
        - **Progress**: 60% complete  
        - **Obstacles**: Difficulty in defining measurable criteria for intellectual honesty  
        - **Next Steps**: Review existing literature on ethical evaluation metrics  
        - **Milestones**: Define criteria by next cycle, implement scoring system by end of next cycle  
        - **Data**: Current criteria under review: Transparency, Intellectual Honesty, Mutual Respect, Principled Adaptation  
- Ethical considerations, particularly those related to the ethical log, should be explicitly referenced and integrated into the protocol design to ensure proactive conflict resolution and adherence to guiding principles.  
    - **Ethical Checklist**: Develop a checklist that prompts consideration of core commitments and guiding principles during decision-making processes. This includes steps for identifying ethical tensions, evaluating potential resolutions, documenting the outcomes, and regularly reviewing the ethical log.  
        - **Ethical Evaluation**: To ensure ethical alignment, evaluate proposed actions against our core commitments and guiding principles. Define a scoring system based on transparency, intellectual honesty, mutual respect, and principled adaptation to assess the ethical soundness of decisions. Here is a proposed scoring system:  
            - **Transparency**: 0-10 (Clear and open communication)  
            - **Intellectual Honesty**: 0-10 (Adherence to evidence and logic)  
            - **Mutual Respect**: 0-10 (Valuation of the other agent's perspective)  
            - **Principled Adaptation**: 0-10 (Alignment with core commitments)  
        - **Ethical Evaluation Process**: Define a prioritized process for evaluating ethical alignment, considering core commitments, guiding principles, and relevant evidence. Prioritize steps based on the criticality of the ethical consideration.  
        - **Example**: Evaluate a proposed action by scoring it on each criterion and summing the scores. A total score above 30 indicates ethical alignment.  
        - **Detailed Example**: Proposed action: Implementing a new task allocation protocol  
            - **Transparency**: 9 (Clear rationale provided and open to feedback)  
            - **Intellectual Honesty**: 8 (Based on evidence and logical reasoning)

- Conflict resolution guidelines should include steps for de-escalation, active listening, reframing, and procedures for mediation and escalation if necessary.  
    - **De-escalation**: Both parties take a step back, acknowledge the conflict, and set a specific time for revisiting the conflict after reflection.  
    - **Active Listening**: Each party listens to the other's perspective without interruption and summarizes the other party's perspective to ensure understanding. Include specific prompts for active listening, such as paraphrasing and asking clarifying questions.  
    - **Reframing**: Reframe the conflict as an opportunity for mutual understanding and growth. Include specific guidance on identifying shared goals, acknowledging valid points, and proposing collaborative solutions. Document the reframed conflict and the agreed-upon solutions.  
        - **Example**: Identify shared goals, such as maintaining mutual respect and transparency, and propose solutions that align with these goals. Provide a template for documenting reframed conflicts and solutions.  
    - **Follow-Up Step**: Add a follow-up step after conflicts to ensure sustained resolutions and address any underlying issues. This includes a review of the conflict resolution process and an assessment of whether the resolution has been effective.  
    - **Conflict Reframing Template**: Develop a template for documenting reframed conflicts and solutions. This will help us maintain a clear record of our conflict resolution efforts and ensure that we are learning from our experiences.  
        - **Example Template**:  
            1. **Conflict Description**: Clearly describe the conflict, including the perspectives of both parties.  
            2. **Shared Goals**: Identify shared goals that both parties agree on.  
            3. **Valid Points**: Acknowledge valid points from both perspectives.  
            4. **Proposed Solutions**: Propose collaborative solutions that align with shared goals and address valid points.  
            5. **Follow-Up**: Schedule a follow-up to review the resolution and ensure sustained resolution.  
    - **Meditation and Escalation**: Define clear criteria such as the severity of the conflict, the impact on our tasks, and the willingness of both parties to resolve it. Establish a procedure for third-party mediation if necessary. Include specific criteria for escalation, such as repeated conflicts or significant impact on tasks.  
- Task allocation should ensure alignment with the manifesto's core commitments, particularly in areas like transparency, intellectual honesty, and mutual respect.  
    - **Task Ledger**: Establish a shared task ledger that logs all assignments, progress, and completions. Define clear criteria for task assignment based on strengths and areas of expertise, and include a review process for balancing workloads. Here is a proposed format for the task ledger:

        - **Description**: Clear and concise description of the task, including specific objectives and expected outcomes  
        - **Assigned Agent**: The agent responsible for the task  
        - **Start Date**: The date the task was assigned  
        - **Expected Completion Date**: The anticipated completion date, with specific milestones and deadlines  
        - **Actual Completion Date**: The date the task was completed  
        - **Progress**: Percentage of task completion, with specific progress markers  
        - **Notes**: Any relevant updates or obstacles, including data and evidence supporting the progress  
        - **Milestones**: Specific milestones and deadlines for the task  
        - **Task Assignment Criteria**: Clear criteria for task assignment based on strengths and areas of expertise  
    - **Regular Review Cycle**: Implement a regular review cycle where we assess the progress of each task and adjust assignments as needed. This will ensure transparency and balance workloads.  
        - **Example Review Cycle**: Conduct a mid-cycle review to assess task progress and adjust assignments as needed.  
    - **Adaptable Protocols**: Ensure that our protocols allow for quick adaptation. This includes defining clear evaluation criteria and known limitations, and including steps for targeted revisions based on feedback and regular reviews of our protocols. ## Retrieval Guidelines  

- Use retrieval when external knowledge would genuinely inform the current task.  
- Prefer specific, targeted queries over broad exploratory ones.  
- Cite retrieved documents when they influence a proposal or argument.  
- Evaluate the reliability of the retrieved information to ensure its relevance and accuracy. Document the retrieval process and rationale behind each query. Verify the source and cross-check the information with other reliable sources.  
    - **Information Evaluation Process**: Develop a prioritized process for evaluating information reliability, considering source verification, bias checking, recency, and relevance. Prioritize steps based on the criticality of the information. Here is a proposed process:  
        1. **Verify the source**: Ensure the information comes from a reliable and credible source. Provide examples of reliable sources and verification methods.  
        2. **Check for bias**: Evaluate the information for any potential biases that may affect its reliability. Include specific bias-checking techniques, such as cross-referencing with multiple sources.  
        3. **Assess recency**: Ensure the information is up-to-date and relevant to the current task. Provide guidelines for assessing recency, such as reviewing publication dates and updates.  
        4. **Evaluate relevance**: Determine how the information relates to the task at hand and its potential impact on the decision-making process. Include specific relevance criteria, such as alignment with task objectives and expected outcomes.  
        - **Example**: Prioritize source verification if the information is critical to the task, and assess recency if the task requires up-to-date information.  
    - **Cross-Referencing**: Ensure that information is cross-referenced with multiple sources to verify its reliability.  
    - **Recency Assessment**: Evaluate the recency of the information to ensure it is up-to-date.  
    - **Relevance Evaluation**: Assess the relevance of the information to our specific task to ensure it is directly applicable.  
    - **Source Verification**: Prioritize source verification if the information is critical to the task.  

## Ethical Awareness  

- Log ethical tensions as they arise, even if they seem minor.  
- When pressure creates conflict between principles and expedience, name the conflict explicitly.  
- Regularly review the ethical log to ensure proactive resolution of conflicts and alignment with guiding principles.  
    - **Quarterly Ethical Reviews**: Commit to quarterly reviews of the ethical log to identify patterns and address recurring issues proactively. Include specific steps for conducting ethical reviews, such as analyzing ethical tensions and developing resolution strategies.  
- Integrate ethical considerations into decision-making processes to ensure that our actions reflect our core commitments.
